# PREREG: does a referent's universe grown through the CLAIM GRAPH nominate its aliases better than the same number of strings, and better than locality? (DRAFT, not frozen, nothing scored)
Written 2026-11-20 by the Janus session at its user's direction ("Claim-graph universe"). It follows ADDENDUM-3 of PREREG-CONTEXT-NOMINATION, which predicted that widening through document adjacency is locality, and said the principle needs a claim-graph test on a task where the DOCUMENT holds the evidence (DIAGNOSTIC-CLAIM-REACH: on row-30 gold, 30 of 58 have no handle and at most 19% have even a possible bond). Rules: REFERENT-FIRST.md. Stamp: sha256 first 16 hex in PREREG-CLAIM-GRAPH-UNIVERSE.md.sha256short, written before any scoring.

## 1. Referent object, evidence, giver (rule 1)
- The being: a MENTION m, a link span in an article's prose. Its identity is the article it links to, which is READ FROM THE PAGE BYTES (the wikitext link target) and is never given to any arm. The universe of m is a set of sentences plus the clauses (claims) it participates in.
- The task (DOC-ALIAS): for each mention m, rank every other mention in the same document; GOLD = mentions with the same link target but a different anchor string. Evidence is inside the document. This is not label-to-page knowledge (rule 4): an arm needs the document's own claims to connect "an invasion" to "Invasion of Iceland".
- Strata: ALIAS (all distinct-anchor same-target pairs) and DISJOINT (the two anchors share no stemmed token under the declared normaliser, so no string handle exists; the primary stratum). DEV census, EXPLORATORY (`aliasgold.py`): 30 documents, 4,773 mentions, 309 alias pairs, 106 DISJOINT pairs (0 to 14 per document). Under 30 would be unjudgeable; 106 is not.
- Evidence a universe may use: the reader's rows (khora-pharos-reading, English parser prior, a given with the treebank as giver), clauses built from the parser's own dependency labels (UD core argument relations: a declared list with UD as the giver, a codec of the parser's output, not a word list), and Janus's `binding` candidates (all candidates, none chosen). No regex, no date or word lists, no model judging.

## 2. Arms (all NOMINATORS: they order attention, never decide sameness; Janus never convicts)
- CG (the claim-graph universe): hop 0 = the sentences and clauses of m and of every mention whose anchor STRING equals m's (a declared string handle, labelled as one; it is the only string handle). Hop h adds the clauses whose participants co-participate, in a clause already in the universe, with a mention already in the universe, ORIENTED upstream: only clauses reached through an argument relation of a member, never through plain adjacency. A candidate mention scores by the best cosine between its own clause sentence and any universe sentence (units kept, no averaging; the embedder is a given from its training: a nominator). Stopping rule: reach is the smallest h >= 1 whose top-10 shares at least TAU = 0.8 of its members with hop h - 1; TAU swept at 0.6 and 1.0; HMAX = 6 is a safety cap only, runs hitting it counted as unstable. TAU = 0.8 is carried from ADDENDUM-3, fixed before any run of this arm.
- CGB = CG plus Janus bindings: pronoun and description anaphors whose candidates include a universe mention add that anaphor's sentence to the universe (all candidates listed, none chosen; number mismatch excludes). It says whether bindings add reach.
- Twins and controls, each reported beside its arm (rule 3):
  - T_CG, the strings-only TWIN: the same hop-0 seeds plus random sentences of the document to the size CG reached, scored identically.
  - ADJ: ADDENDUM-3's adjacency universe (N6) on this task, same TAU. CG must beat adjacency, or claims added nothing over proximity.
  - PROX: nearest-sentence distance to a hop-0 seed (no model).
  - A0: bare anchor string embedded (the strings baseline; the label handle).
  - Target-swap: the same arm run with m replaced by a random other mention. The ratio is the topical-versus-referent-tied check.
  - FLAT: the clause graph with its argument labels shuffled within each document (structure removed, strings kept). It says whether the DEPENDENCY structure carries the gain.

## 3. Falsifiers, written first (document-bootstrap interval of the paired difference above 0 and a difference of at least 0.05 recall@10 on DISJOINT, as in the parent)
- G1: CG does not beat A0 (claims add nothing over the label). G2: CG does not beat T_CG (structure adds nothing over the same strings). G3: CG does not beat ADJ (the claim graph is no better than adjacency). G4: CG does not beat PROX (locality). G5: CG does not beat FLAT (dependency labels add nothing: it was strings). G6: CG target-swap ratio is 0.5 or more (topical). G7: CGB does not beat CG (bindings add no reach). G8: precision@10 of CG under half of A0's (gain bought with junk).
- Honest reading rule: if G2, G3, G4 and G5 are all NOT hit, the claim "a referent's universe is its claims, grown until the next step makes no difference, nominates its aliases better than its strings" has survived on this task for English and this reader; if any is hit it names which part was strings. No claim beyond the DISJOINT stratum, one language, one reader. A positive result is a nomination result: sameness is still declared by a giver (rule 10), never by this score.

## 5. What this does not test
Whether a model-based fold engine does better (it reports differences with witnesses and never judges: rule 10 RULED 4); pronoun binding accuracy (GAP gold, a download the owner has not approved); other languages; any claim about the DEF of identity itself. The claim graph here uses string-equal anchors as its only hop-0 handle, so a referent with a single mention and no alias statement has no universe: that limit is stated, not hidden.

## 6. Declarations made while building, BEFORE any FRESH read (DEV was run three times for debugging; no DEV number is claimed and nothing below was tuned to move a score)
1. **Nodes.** The claim graph's nodes are anchor classes (the link-mention string handle) and LEMMA nodes (the second declared string handle: a non-mention common or proper noun that is a UD core argument, keyed by the reader's lemma). Without lemma nodes, 5,882 of 13,117 clauses would not exist: the first build had 1.09 participants per clause and could not grow. Pronouns are left to the binding evidence.
2. **Growth.** U_0 = {the class}; C_h = clauses touching U_h; U_{h+1} = U_h plus the participants of C_h; the universe sentences P_h = the class's own sentences plus the sentences of C_h. CGB adds the sentences of anaphors whose binding candidates (number not a mismatch) include a class in U_h.
3. **Stopping rule over distinct universes.** A hop that adds no sentence changes nothing by construction, so it is skipped and never counts as stability; a universe that stops growing is exhausted (stable); growth still going at HMAX is unstable.
4. **What DEV showed about reach (stated, not claimed).** 317 of 416 query classes never grew past their own sentences (the mention is not a core argument of any clause, or its clauses lie in its own sentence), so for them the twin equals the arm and G2 cannot discriminate. Therefore the stratum **GROWN** (the universe grew beyond its own sentences) and **GROWN_DISJOINT** are declared; **G2 to G5 and G7 are judged on GROWN_DISJOINT when it holds at least 30 gold, and on DISJOINT they are reported but cannot speak for the universe**, because most queries there have no universe. DEV had 29 gold in GROWN_DISJOINT (unjudgeable by one).
5. **Honest reading of DEV, stated before the fresh read.** On DEV every falsifier except G6-on-DISJOINT and G8 was hit, and on the grown subset adjacency beat the claim graph (0.448 vs 0.138 on 29 gold, unjudgeable). The expectation for the fresh read is the same: the principle's claim graph, as the reader's core-argument clauses give it, is too sparse and too noisy to beat adjacency or the bare label. A hit is a finding about this reader's claim extraction (one English parser prior), not a refutation of the principle; the experiment says what a better extraction would have to deliver: growth for more than a quarter of the classes, and a gain over adjacency where it grows.
