# PREREG: what PRIORS would mechanically recover the answer the gold is confident about where our parser and the teacher disagree, held as a spectrum until the context is enough to collapse? (frozen before any table is mined or any token scored; nothing run)
Written 2026-11-20 by the Janus session at its user's direction ("for where there is misalignment, lets find what priors we need to mechanically get the answer that the golden seems confident on, and lets hold things in eigenvalues until we get enough context to collapse"). The stance is Janus's own for binding (binding-evidence.mjs lists every candidate with its raw facts and picks none) and Penelope's for commitment (anaphor-commit.mjs: a candidate leaves only on a declared checkable fact; a weight comes from the structure; commitment happens at use). Stamp: PREREG-PRIOR-COLLAPSE.md.sha256short.

## 1. Material and labels
The DELTA: every non-punctuation token where the teacher (Stanza 1.15.1, gold tokens in) and our pooled parser disagree on (head, relation): LinES test and ParTUT test (3,176 tokens; REUSED: their POOL and STANZA numbers were seen) and, as DEV, EWT dev and GUM dev (Stanza possibly contaminated there). For each such token the SPECTRUM has two candidates: A = the teacher's (head, relation), B = ours. A token is REACHABLE if the gold's (head, relation) equals A or B (the gold is confident in one of them); on LinES and ParTUT that is 1,874 + 471 = 2,345 of 3,176 (74%); the other 831 are unreachable by any choice between these two and are reported, not scored.
Priors are MINED from the GOLD TRAIN splits only (EWT train and GUM train, the data our parsers trained on; GUM is non-commercial: only tables of counts, kept local, nothing committed): they are structural likelihood tables with the treebank as giver and standing CANDIDATE (a corpus can refute and never earn): P(relation | the token's UPOS); P(head UPOS | relation); P(direction of the head | relation); P(distance bucket | relation); P(the arc crosses another arc | relation). No word, no regex. Smoothing: add-one (alpha = 1, an ARBITRARY CONSTANT of ours, labelled). The token's UPOS and the candidate head's UPOS are the TEACHER's tags (both candidates share them: the question is the arc, not the tag). The CROSSING feature is computed against the CONSENSUS arcs: the arcs of the sentence where both parsers agree on (head, relation).

## 2. The spectrum and the collapse
For each candidate the log2 weight is the sum over the five priors of log2 P(its value). The MARGIN is log2 weight(A) minus log2 weight(B); the normalized weights w = 2^s / (2^sA + 2^sB) are carried on the token: BOTH candidates stay in the spectrum. A token COLLAPSES to a candidate only at use, when |margin| is at least m, m being SWEPT over 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4 (no cut-off is chosen: the curve is the result); below m it stays HELD. Reported per m: coverage (the share of reachable tokens collapsed), precision (the collapsed candidate equals the gold), and the baselines on the SAME collapsed tokens: always the teacher, always ours.

## 3. Arms
PRIORS (all five). ABLATION: each prior alone and each left out. TWIN (rule 3): the same tables mined from gold train with the RELATION LABELS shuffled among arcs (seeded): the priors with the relation information destroyed. VETO: among reachable tokens where the priors prefer OUR candidate at margin m, how often is ours right versus the teacher.

## 4. Falsifiers, written first
- C1: at no m (from 1 up) does PRIORS' precision on its collapsed tokens exceed always-the-teacher's precision on the same tokens (the priors add nothing beyond believing the teacher).
- C2: at no m does the VETO show ours right more often than the teacher on the tokens where the priors prefer ours (the priors cannot find the teacher's errors).
- C3: the TWIN's precision at m = 1 is not below PRIORS' by 5 points (the priors are not carrying relation-specific structure; 5 is an ARBITRARY CONSTANT, labelled).
- C4: PRIORS' precision at m = 4 is not above its precision at m = 0 (more evidence does not make the collapse better: the spectrum has no signal to wait for).
- Reading rule: the priors are useful to HOLD-AND-COLLAPSE only if C1 or C2 is NOT hit and C3 and C4 are NOT hit. A hit names what the priors did not do. The ablation says which prior carries it; nothing here is `given`: the tables are candidates; any rule a person or standard declares (a UD guideline: a case marker attaches to its noun) is a separate DEF with a named giver, Penelope's.

## 5. Limits and rules
English; two candidates per token (the two nominations; the true head may be a third); five priors only; one treebank family as giver; REUSED labels as stated.
