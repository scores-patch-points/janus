# DESIGN (a proposal for the owner, Penelope and khora; nothing here is built): pronoun coreference rules held by a giver, scoped by language, variety and period, induced emergently from goldens, and loaded with the codec at read time
Written 2026-11-20 by the Janus session at its user's direction: rules on pronoun coreference that come from a giver per language, with provenance and a sense of the language and period they apply to; the same rules extracted emergently wherever we have goldens; all of it online when something is parsed from a given codec; English is not one codec; there should be a distribution of information about everything, and the standard English codec is what gets folded at read time. Every statement about what exists was checked on 2026-11-20 against the file named beside it.

## 1. What exists, and where it falls short of that
- Codec registry (`priors/ud-codecs.json`, 95 entries): ONE codec per ISO 639-3 code. English is `eng` = UD_English-EWT only; the family keys `ang`, `enm`, `emo`, `sco` are absent. Each entry carries treebank, licence, source file and sha256. So "English" is one treebank's web text; no variety, genre or period axis exists.
- Pronoun priors (`priors/pronoun-en.json`, `-grc`, `-ru`): measured forms with provenance (source treebank, licence, builder, tokens read), again one source per language and no period.
- khora `adapters/text/pronoun-registers.js`: a register per language STEM (`stemKey` strips everything after "-" or "_", so a variety tag collapses to the language); it ships only a register that carries the evaluation it was measured by ("no unmeasured binding ships") and files typed gaps (`pronoun_presence_no_register`, `pronoun_presence_unmeasured`) instead of applying English silently. English is the built-in class with dials. That discipline is right and should be kept.
- Penelope `organs/anaphor-commit.mjs`: three declared rules (gender-clash, non-nominal, disjoint-reference) written as code. Each rule is a fact about English UD rows with no language, variety, period or giver attached; the elimination text names the rule, not who gave it or where it holds.
- Janus `binding-evidence.mjs`: lists every candidate with raw facts and picks none; that division stays.
- Today's GAP debugging run: one deterministic parse, one codec, three rules that remove almost nothing (candidates are the same gender by design; English names carry no Gender in the codec). It cannot show whether layers help, because there are no layers.

## 2. The proposal in five parts
### 2.1 A rule is a record with a giver, a scope and standing (a DEF at Pattern: a prior, never a thing Janus writes)
`CorefRule@1 = { id, effect, language, variety, period, scope, giver, standing, evidence, provenance }`
- effect: what it does to the candidate set, in the vocabulary Penelope already has: ELIMINATE (a candidate leaves because of a declared checkable fact), PERMIT (a configuration that allows coreference where another rule would forbid it: reflexives, possessives, adjuncts), PREFER is NOT an effect (activation never decides: her ruling). A rule never chooses.
- language: ISO 639-3. variety: a named codec variety (see 2.2). period: `{ from, to, basis }` with the basis stated (the treebank's date range, a grammar's own claim, a standard's edition); unknown period is `null`, never "now".
- scope: the pronoun class and construction it covers (he-class non-possessive core argument; reflexive; possessive determiner; pro-drop subject in a case-marked language), so a rule cannot leak onto a construction it was not stated for.
- giver: who says so, as a name and a kind: `grammar` (a reference grammar or the UD guidelines' binding principles, cited), `treebank` (a gold-annotated corpus, with its release), `standard` (a declared convention), `asker` (the owner or a person's declaration), or `emergent` (induced here, see 2.3). The grain theorem (khora hl-acquire.js) holds: a corpus can REFUTE a universal rule and never earn it, so an `emergent` rule is at most standing `candidate`; only a named giver makes it `given`.
- evidence: the goldens it was checked on, as ids and counts, the split labels (DEV, FRESH, HELD-OUT, REUSED), precision where it removes a candidate and how often it removed a RIGHT one (the harm), the null it was compared with.
- provenance: the chain from giver to this record (who wrote it into the prior, when, from which file hash).
A rule with no giver, no language or no scope is refused at load (a typed gap), never defaulted.

### 2.2 English is a set of codecs, and the read folds them
- Codecs are keyed by (language, variety, period), not by language: for English that means at least the registered EWT (web text), GUM (multi-genre; khora already evaluates on it) and any other UD English treebank we hold or point at, and, where a historical treebank exists, its own entries (the registry holds none; that is a typed gap, not an English default). I have not re-verified the current list of UD English treebanks.
- Each codec contributes its own distributions: feature and relation frequencies, the pronoun register, and the rules whose scope names it. Nothing is averaged into one table at build time.
- At read time the reader FOLDS. A text arrives with whatever is known (declared variety and date, else nothing). The fold weights the codecs' distributions by what is known (a declared period selects; unknown spreads over the varieties, with the "standard English" fold as the default face when nothing is known) and returns BOTH the folded reading and the layers it came from with their weights. The standard English codec is the default fold of the distribution, computed on read, never the one stored truth. A face that wants the unfolded layers (Penelope holding a spectrum, an EVA testing agreement across independent codecs) takes them.
- Where layers agree, that agreement is standing across independent sources (the EVA·Pattern design, DESIGN-EVA-PATTERN-STANDING.md); where they disagree, the disagreement is evidence Janus structures and Penelope judges.

### 2.3 Emergent rules from goldens (structure only; standing candidate)
Wherever a gold exists (GAP, UD GUM and other coreference corpora, PROIEL for Greek, a treebank with coreference for another language), induce candidate rules from the gold, not from strings:
- Features are structural and language-neutral: the dependency relations of pronoun and candidate, whether they share a predicate, c-command-like attachment paths, sentence distance, cube-addressed agreement features (a hole when absent), nominality. No word list and no regex.
- The induced object is a CONFIGURATION that never co-occurs with gold coreference ("candidate and pronoun are both core arguments of one predicate"), reported with its exposure count n and its violations. By the grain theorem it is `candidate` while violations are zero and exposure is at or above a floor; one violation refutes it (khora's functional induction has the same three standings: unexposed, refuted, candidate). Nothing is `given` by induction.
- Validity tests, written before running: (a) REDISCOVERY: does the induction recover the given disjoint-reference rule from GAP development rows with no hint of it? (b) CROSS-SPLIT: do candidate rules induced on development survive validation and the single test read? (c) CROSS-VARIETY: does a rule induced on one codec's text survive on another's (EWT, GUM)? The rules that survive across varieties are the ones whose scope can be widened; the ones that do not carry their variety in their scope. (d) HARM: how often does a rule remove a gold-correct candidate.
- Language by language the same procedure runs on that language's gold; the given rules for that language come from its grammar or treebank with the giver named, and emergent candidates are compared with them. Where there is no gold, the language has given rules or none (a typed gap), never induced ones.

### 2.4 It comes online with the codec
`loadGrammar(code, { variety, period })` (janus `nl-eot.mjs`, the codec door) returns the grammar AND its rules and registers for that scope, each with its record, or the typed gaps `no_grammar`, `no_rules`, `rule_unmeasured`. Every elimination Penelope writes cites the rule id, its giver, its language, variety and period. A text read with no matching rule set is HELD, never bound by another language's or period's rules.

### 2.5 Who writes what
Janus writes structure and the induction harness's counts (Existence and Structure cells only); it never writes a rule and never convicts. Penelope writes the rules as DEF·Pattern priors with named givers (given), and the emergent candidates only as `candidate`; she judges (EVA) and revises (REC). khora owns the codecs, registers and the read-time fold. No repo imports another: the rule records travel as log entries and files read through each repo's door.

2. CODEC-VARIETY: build GUM-derived and EWT-derived registers for English with scope tags, fold them at read time, and test whether the fold beats either alone on documents of both kinds, against a strings-only twin (a fold over shuffled varieties).
3. LAYERS-ON-GAP: the layered-readings arm for the pronoun task (several codecs and the rows-level reading as layers; a candidate survives when the layers agree), with the single-parse result of PREREG-GAP-PRONOUN-EOT as its baseline.
